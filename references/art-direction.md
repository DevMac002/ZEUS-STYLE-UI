# Art direction

## Principle

The right style is the style that expresses the product's identity and helps its audience understand and use it. Do not default to one fashionable aesthetic.

## Evaluate

1. Domain conventions and audience expectations.
2. Brand personality and supplied brand rules.
3. Content type, density, and hierarchy.
4. Desired emotional tone and differentiation.
5. Technical constraints and asset availability.
6. Accessibility and responsive behavior.

## Concept generation

For new or ambiguous work, usually present 2–3 concepts. Each should differ in composition or design logic, not merely hue. Describe:
- Design thesis and intended audience response.
- Layout and information hierarchy.
- Typography and type scale.
- Palette and contrast strategy.
- Image/illustration treatment.
- Component shape and density.
- Motion/interaction approach.
- Benefits, trade-offs, and implementation implications.

For a small or tightly constrained change, recommend one direction. Adapt concept count to the actual decision complexity.

## Avoid generic output

Avoid repeating the same hero, floating gradient blobs, glassmorphism, rounded cards, icon tiles, or testimonial strips without a reason. Use cards only when grouping content benefits comprehension. Avoid decorative complexity that competes with the primary task.

## Reference analysis

When given screenshots or URLs, identify transferable principles: spacing, hierarchy, layout, typography, color relationships, content density, and interactions. Separate observed facts from guesses. Respect trademarks, copyright, licensing, and asset provenance; use references as guidance rather than assuming permission to reproduce proprietary assets.

## Visual acceptance

Define what success looks like before implementation: hierarchy, readability, brand fit, responsive composition, component consistency, and relevant interaction states.
